    1. Save your own copy first, so you always have a clean one.
    2. On My investments, enter one row for every payment: the date, Money in or Money out from the list, and the amount as a plain positive number.
    3. In the last row enter today’s date, choose Worth today, and type what the holding is worth now.
    4. If a date reads back as a different day, your computer is set to month-first dates; retype it the other way round.

    About this tool

    Your data

    Everything you type or load is worked out inside your browser, on your device. Nothing is uploaded, stored on a server, or written to this device. Close the tab and it is gone, which is why there is a button to save your own entries as a file.

    Where does the arithmetic run?
    In this page, on your device.
    What does this page request?
    Only its own files, from this address. Nothing from any other site, at any point.
    Where do a fund’s or an index’s prices come from?
    A file you download and choose yourself.
    Are password-protected files read?
    No. There is no decryption here.
    What is sent anywhere?
    Nothing.
    Is there an account, a sign-up or an email?
    None.
    What do the figures leave out?
    Exit load and tax. A fund’s NAV already includes its expense ratio.

    How this tool calculates

    • A year is 365 days, the convention of spreadsheet XIRR and of every factsheet.
    • A window of N years ends on the same calendar date N years later, or on the last value up to seven days before it; beyond that the window is dropped, never stretched. Each window’s figure is the value at its end divided by the value at its start, raised to the power 365 divided by its days, minus 1.
    • Weekly and monthly start frequencies thin the start dates only; every window is still measured first value to last.
    • Percentiles use linear interpolation between neighbours, the spreadsheet PERCENTILE rule. Where your own stretch sits among a fund’s stretches of the same length is the share of those stretches that returned less than the fund did over your dates, ties counted half.
    • The same rupees in the index: each payment buys index units at the index value on that date, or the last value on or before it; withdrawals sell units; the units left are valued on the valuation date.
    • Units from a NAV file: amount divided by the NAV on the payment date, or the next NAV on or after it.
    • Volatility is the standard deviation of day-to-day log returns times the square root of the observations per year in the file.
    • Capture ratios use month-end values. The information ratio uses daily arithmetic returns and the standard deviation of daily excess returns, the basis SEBI set in circular SEBI/HO/IMD/IMD-PoD-2/P/CIR/2025/6 of 17 January 2025.
    • Dates are read day first; a file whose dates only make sense month first is read month first; a file that reads both ways says so.
    • Rows with no value, a zero, or a negative are counted and shown, never used. The same date twice keeps the last. Gaps wider than 45 days are reported.
    • Everything is before exit load and tax; a fund’s NAV already carries its expense ratio.

    What this is

    A companion to the book, for readers who want to see their own position and understand every number in it without needing anyone else. It measures money that has already moved and illustrates assumptions you choose yourself. It fetches nothing, so nothing in it goes stale: the history is the file you bring.

    What this is not

    It does not recommend funds, rate them, rank them, or tell you what to buy, sell, hold or switch. Figures are before exit load and before tax. It is an educational tool, not investment advice.

    Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully. Past performance may or may not be sustained in future.

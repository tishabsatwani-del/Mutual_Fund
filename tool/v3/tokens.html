Colours

Two sheets, following the phone's own day and night setting, with no toggle. Text pairs need 4.5:1; a rule that is the edge of something tappable needs 3:1; a rule that only divides may be quiet. Each token is measured against the ground it actually sits on — the reader's own figures against the composited band by day, button text against ink — because a ratio taken against a colour the pair never meets on screen proves nothing.

The rules that are not values

  • No red and no green anywhere, the riskometer's colours included. A return is ink or it is marker.
  • Marker means "you", and nothing else is ever this colour. The marker is never a button, because it means you, not press.
  • Slate is the comparison series — the index fund's line and its figure — and nothing else.
  • Nothing is a gradient and nothing glows. Buttons are ink on paper by day and paper on ink by night.
  • No cards inside cards. Exactly two boxed things in the whole product: the ledger and the reading.
  • One primary action per screen; every other action is a text link.
  • A refusal is set like a reading — serif sentence, hairline, next step — never a red box with an exclamation mark. The tool declining to guess is part of its character, not an error state.
  • Never on screen: scientific notation, more than two decimals on a rupee figure, a number wider than its container, or a rupee figure with no ₹.
  • States to design, not just the happy one: empty, partly filled, computing, result, refusal (fund too young, gap, no overlap, out-of-range input), and the age guard.
